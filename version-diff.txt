Added Exo-Mastery, conflict zones, the EDDN relay and journal-learned names

---

Exo-Mastery: where the exobiology money is
- Added an opt-in Exo-Mastery catalogue on the Commander tab: one button downloads a published list of star systems whose planets carry high-value exobiology (reduced from Spansh: species worth more than ten million credits, within a thousand light years of Sol) and shows the totals - systems, bodies, credits on offer and credits already harvested. Disable unloads it again after a confirmation.
- Added "take me to the next exobiology site": VEGA plots a route to the richest catalogued system that still has an unsampled body in it, announces how many planets and how much they are worth, and leaves an arrival reminder naming the planets to land on. If the best system is the one you are already in, it names the planets still open here instead.
- Added "we have done this whole system already": writes off every catalogued body in the current system for commanders who sampled it before the app was installed, naming the bodies and the credits written off. The per-planet correction (marking one body's survey complete) now also accepts a body that is only known from the catalogue.
- Bodies are ticked off automatically as you play: the third scan of the last listed species on a body completes it, and so does the game's own survey-complete signal, whoever triggers it. Completed bodies survive disabling and re-enabling the catalogue, so a later Enable never sends you back to a planet the game will not let you sample twice.

Conflict zones and combat bonds
- Added a conflict-zone ledger, the twin of the hunting-ground one for the other kind of fighting: every FSS sweep files the war zones a system holds (low, medium, high intensity, and powerplay zones counted separately) and every arrival files who is fighting there, or that nobody is any more. A war not sighted for a week is treated as over.
- Added "find me a conflict zone" (combat bonds): VEGA names the best war within the range you asked for - the one sighted most recently, then the hardest fighting, then the nearest - tells you the sides where they are known, and plots the route.
- Added a conflict-zone HUD card: which zone you dropped into, the combat bonds earned so far, the kill count and the side paying you. It stays up while you fly to the station to cash in and clears when the combat bonds are redeemed; cashing bounties or codex vouchers at the same counter leaves it alone.
- Combat bonds (FactionKillBond) are now read from the journal, silently - kills in a zone come every few seconds and the card carries the tally, so nothing is spoken per kill.

The EDDN relay
- Added a listener for the EDDN relay, the public feed of other commanders' journal events. It runs silently in the background from app start and feeds only the hunting-ground and conflict-zone ledgers, so the app knows where the resource sites and the wars are long before you have flown there yourself. Strictly subscribe-only: nothing of yours is ever uploaded. If the connection drops it reconnects on its own, and if it cannot, the app is whole without it and just learns more slowly.
- The arrival announcement of a hunting ground when you are in a system your pirate contracts point at has been removed: the ledgers now fill up quietly from your own scans and the relay, and you read them back by asking for a hunting ground or a war zone.

Materials and commodities learned from the journal
- The app now learns materials and commodities it has never heard of - the new ones a game update brings - from your own journal, the first time they appear on a market board, in the hold, on a prospector reading or out of the refinery. From then on they can be asked about by name, set as mining targets and named when prospected, without waiting for an app update.
- The learned name is filed under the language your game client runs in, never guessed into another, so it is always the word on your HUD. A commander on a non-English client hears the client's word for it even with the app in another language, and the app understands that word whatever language they speak to it in.
- A good learned on a non-English client has no English trade name yet, which is what the market search needs, so asking where to buy or sell it gets a plain "I know it from the journal but not its trade name yet" rather than a false "not found".
- A learned material's storage cap is not known (the journal never states it), so VEGA now states the amount held and says nothing about capacity rather than announcing a capacity of zero.

Journal reading
- Fixed the choice of which journal file to follow: it is now decided from the start stamp in the file's name rather than its modification time. Windows reports a stale time for the file the game is writing, which could make the app tail a dead journal for a whole session, pre-scan the wrong two files, or put yesterday's journal in a support bundle. The parser also re-checks the folder by name every few seconds as a backstop for a missed file-created notification.
- Fixed the historical mission scan, which was reading the four oldest journals in the folder rather than the four newest - on a long-lived install those never held a live mission.

Construction sites and the HUD
- Fixed the construction card on a stocking leg: with a carrier parked elsewhere holding goods for the build, the card used to list only the goods already complete on the carrier and none of the ones still to buy. It now lists what is still to acquire, largest requirement first, and the loading order no longer tells you to fill the hold with a good the carrier already holds in full.
- The construction card no longer disappears the moment the hold covers everything the site still wants; it stays through the delivery leg showing what is aboard for the site, and the build's completion is what ends it.
- Fixed a construction site freezing at its last good manifest: a stored location row an older build had written could fail to read, and that failure stopped the manifest from being saved on every docking after it. The manifest is now stored regardless, without a name if need be.

Settings
- Split the cloud voice choice into its own switch: Google (needs a key) or Microsoft Edge (keyless), each showing only its own settings, mirroring the local Kokoro or Supertonic switch.
- Removed the "exit UI before opening another panel" toggle. Backing out of an open panel before opening the next is now unconditional: with it off, three back presses were not enough to leave the galaxy map, FSS or station services, the next panel opened on a stale focus and every keystroke after it went astray.

Support bundles and logs
- Added a hardware inventory to the support bundle manifest: CPU model and core count, physical memory, JVM heap, graphics cards with VRAM as the OS reports it, the exact NVIDIA figure from nvidia-smi where it is installed, and free disk space under the install and journal folders. Every probe is guarded on its own so a failing query costs one line, never the bundle.
- The log folder is now capped at the current log plus the three newest rolled files; it used to grow by up to seven files a day for ever.
- A subscriber that throws while handling an event is now written to the app log with the event and the cause. Previously the report went to a channel a Windows install never shows, so a component failing on every event could leave nothing in a bundle at all.
- The journal reader now logs which file it chose, when it switches, and when it is waiting for the game; a construction site's manifest changing and the EDDN listener's connection state are logged too, so a bundle can answer "was the journal being read at all".
